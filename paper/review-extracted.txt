Typed Non-Laundering Authorization for Signed
Deterministic-Program Envelopes
Anonymous Author(s)
Abstract
We study authenticated recovery of signed deterministic pro-
grams over channels that do not preserve bytes. We separate
signed programs from signed execution envelopes and define
a typed, machine-checked non-laundering model over verifier-
aggregated report sets. Policy can grant reasoned origin-only
use, but cannot rename evidence or derive execution-agreement
authority from missing, incomparable, or disagreeing execution.
Independence policy receives only an eight-bit verified projection;
aDistinctaxis qualifies only with registry-verified, externally
attested, or verifier-established provenance and its required
evidence reference.
On a bounded surface of the real deterministic DSL L, a
160/160oracle separated program-only A0, authentication-only
A1, final-result re-execution B, and transcript re-execution C.
Observation-divergent stale-3 is unconstructible at A0, missed
at A1, and detected throughout B/C. Four programs through
five paths produced byte-identical canonical transcripts in fresh-
process reruns; a distinct cross-language backend detected one
controlled Rust-runtime common-mode fault missed by shared-
host paths. The result demonstrates bounded backend separation,
not source-independent replication.

1 Introduction
Authenticated origin must be separated from decision authority.
For signed deterministic programs, a recipient must distinguish the
exact program and context, an attributed execution claim, local de-
terministic agreement with that claim, and authority for an action.
A signature authenticates the claim rather than proving that the
computation occurred or was correct. Re-execution supplies only
case-specific agreement, and shared faults or unsupported profiles
remain possible. Treating these states as interchangeable launders
authority.
DSSE, in-toto, SLSA, and Sigstore establish exact-message or
provenance facts [6, 9, 10, 12], but not verifier-side execution agree-
ment or the later authorization decision. We encode that down-
stream non-laundering boundary in a typed state model and test
the incremental detection value of re-execution on real engines.
SignedProgramV1binds canonical L IR, semantics, and context.
SignedExecutionEnvelopeV1adds a versioned engine-attributed
claim. The verifier authenticates exact bytes under external trust
policy, checks context, and conditionally re-executes L.Verifi
cationStatusrecords terminal execution evidence andAuthor
izationBasisa later grant without rewriting it. Authorization
consumes the complete current-report aggregate for one enve-
lope, context, scope, and epoch. Disagreement dominates fallback,
while non-execution and incomparability retain typed origin-only
reasons. The structural theorem covers any predicate over the
verified Boolean projection. The executable model checks 1,024
conjunctive-monotone policies and three laundering mutations.
The real-engine matrix spans four families and five paths on a
bounded L surface. I3 compares the native reference with a cross-
language transliteration that shares source, parser, and semantic-
core lineage but separates runtime backend and build toolchain.
Across 160 cells, observation-divergent stale-3 isN/Aat A0, missed
at A1, and detected by B/C. A controlled Rust-host common-mode
fault is missed at I1/I2 and detected by E4 at I3. This tests backend,
not source-lineage, separation. The evaluation states all coverage
and independence limits.

This paper makes two contributions in claim order:
(1)A typed, machine-checked non-laundering disci-
pline.Split signed types, complete-set joins, typed fall-

3.2 Meaning of the execution claim
Anengine-attributed execution claimis a publisher-signed
statement that a particular observation tuple is attributed to
the implementation described byattributed_engine.Verified
execution agreementis the verifier’s finding that its determin-
istic re-execution yields the same committed observation. An
execution proofwould verify computation correctness without
re-execution. This construction supplies the first notion and can
derive the second; it supplies no execution proof.
The signature makes the attribution immutable as part of
𝑒. It does not attest the occurrence of publisher-side execu-
tion. Operationally, the commitment is a promotion contract:
execution-agreement authority is unavailable unless a comparable
verifier-side re-execution agrees with the signed observation. The
comparison can expose version drift, a newly signed stale claim,
or implementation divergence, while remaining agnostic about
whether the publisher obtained the observation by execution,
prediction, or another process.
attributed_enginehas exactly two meanings: the signer at-
tributes the signed execution claim to the identified implementa-
tion, and the descriptor supplies publisher-asserted inputs for con-
structing anIndependenceProfile. Its signature proves only that
those inputs are unchanged. It does not prove their independence
assertions, attest that the described binary existed at a place or
time, or show that it was loaded, executed this IR, produced the
claim, had a trustworthy build, or returned a correct result. Sec-
tion 4.1 therefore requires independent provenance before an axis
can support high-authority promotion.
3.3 Stale claims and bound fields
ForSignedExecutionEnvelopeV1, the whole-object signature dis-
tinguishes three stale cases:
(1)stale-1:attaching a result digest from a different envelope
to the current IR, or otherwise mixing an IR and result from
different envelopes, changes signed bytes and fails authen-
tication;
(2)stale-2:replaying an old but valid whole envelope does
not forge a signature and is addressed, when required, by
signed purpose, subject, nonce, expiry, and freshness pol-
icy; and
(3)stale-3:signing an old result as a new claim for the current
IR can pass authentication and freshness. Re-execution de-
tects such a freshly signed stale claim when its committed
observation differs from the current deterministic execu-
tion. A stale claim that remains observation-equal under
its commitment scope is not detected by this comparison.
Full intermediate-trace differences are detectable only when the
versionedcommitment_scopeincludes those observation axes. A
final-result commitment makes no completeness claim about un-
committed intermediate behavior.

8.2 RQ3: re-execution adds detection that
signed IR alone lacks
The mechanical oracle passed160/160 oracle cells. A second
fresh-process full run reproduced all six result artifacts byte-for-
byte, including the 16 common-mode Rust-runtime-backend cells.
Four real programs across five execution paths producedbyte-
identical canonical transcripts. Every path reproduced its own
canonical and raw observation bytes exactly in that second fresh
process, confirming canonical determinism for the tested corpus.
Raw observations, as disclosed above, were reproducible per path
but not byte-identical across engines.
Table cells name the detecting layer.PASSis a clean verifica-
tion control. Under A0,N/Amarks an execution-claim attack that
the signedProgrampayload structurally cannot contain. Under A1,
MISSmarks an authenticated signed claim whose divergence re-
mains undetected because re-execution is disabled. This RQ3 mech-
anism harness stops atVerificationStatus; it does not instan-
tiate the verifier-store aggregation or action-promotion decision,
which is evaluated separately by the finite model.
SIG,FRESH, andREEXECare verifier-reported detecting layers:
signature, nonce/freshness, and deterministic re-execution agree-
ment. The fault names are controlled harness ground truth, not
diagnoses emitted by the verifier.
The load-bearing comparison is stale-3. Section 3.3 defines it as
an old result signed as a new execution claim. A0 has no claim field
or consumer action for that result, so stale-3 is structurally uncon-
structible and correctlyN/A. A1 carries the staleSignedExecution
EnvelopeV1and authenticates its atomic binding, but re-execution
is disabled, so every A1 stale-3 cell is a formally validMISS. B and
C caught all injected stale-3 cells atI0–I3because every commit-
ted stale observation differed from current deterministic execution.
This does not generalize to an observation-equal stale claim. The
A1-to-B/C comparison therefore isolates verifier-side re-execution
value without redefining the attack, while A0-to-A1 isolates addi-
tion and signed binding of the claim.
Independence changes which engine faults are visible only
when re-execution occurs. A0 has no claim and those cells are
N/A; A1 carries each divergent claim but misses it throughout.
Under B and C, the controlled single-operation backend fault was
missed by I0, whose publisher and verifier reran the same affected
artifact, and caught byI1–I3. Semantics-version drift had the
same profile. The added engine-side common-mode fault further
separates the profiles: E1/E2/E3 share an activated off-by-one in a
Rust host primitive, so B/C at I1 and I2 agree on the same wrong
result and miss it, while E4’s unmodified cross-language backend
disagrees and detects it in I3. This demonstrates measured value
from runtime-backend independence on one controlled cell. It
doesnotdemonstrate source-lineage independence: E4 is an
engineered transliteration, so a defect in the shared semantic
choices or transliterated evaluator logic could be inherited and
missed. The cell also does not show that the injected defect occurs
naturally or that agreement excludes common defects.
Commitment scope is also material. B cannot observe an equal-
result divergence confined to the available canonical event trace;
C caught that mutation at I0–I3. Because C is not a language-level
step trace, the claim is specifically about the committed observable
root/termination events, not intermediate reduction equivalence.

9 Threat-Model Boundaries and Non-Claims
The following boundaries are part of the result, not deferred imple-
mentation caveats:
•Execution assurance.The envelope supports an attrib-
uted claim and verifier agreement, not proof that the en-
gine ran, an execution or historical proof such as a SNARK,
STARK, or TEE attestation, or defect exclusion. Agreement
only adds differential sensitivity. The games do not esti-
mate attack prevalence.
•Independence.I3 separates runtime and toolchain but
shares transliterated source, parser, and semantic core.
Every path shares operator, organization, machine, and
specification. It is not source, organization, operator, hard-
ware, specification, or publisher-environment replication.
Strength follows the actual vector, and I0–I3 are not ranks.
•Semantic scope.Claims cover canonical L IR, not source-
language equivalence. Four programs and six fixtures oc-
cupy84/205hosted rows. The 160 cells are conditions, and
34/144is only broader historical agreement. This is no
conformance, diversity, whole-language, arbitrary-native-
code, or whole-program result. Canonical equality is not
raw equality or completeness for omitted axes. Exhaustion
equivalence is unproved, and scope C has no language-
level step trace.
•Trust infrastructure.An embedded key is not a trust root.
External trust, profile, semantics, decoder, and verifier poli-
cies are assumed correct. We do not solve policy correct-
ness, key custody, or revocation. Replay depends on exter-
nal enforcement of signed freshness. The system supplies
no log or trusted timestamp. Offline means no online man-
ifest or sidecar, not no signature infrastructure.
•Carrier.Robust mode authenticates recovered envelope
bytes, not received pixels.
10 Related Work
Exact-byte provenance and supply chains.DSSE authenticates
serialized payload bytes, in-toto binds supply-chain steps and arti-
facts, SLSA standardizes provenance, and Sigstore makes software-
signing identities and events auditable [6, 9, 10, 12]. We use that
evidence substrate for a downstream typed authorization machine
and do not replace its trust, identity, revocation, or transparency
functions.
Information flow, typestate, and attestation.Information-
flow lattices constrain flows between labels, typestate permits op-
erations only from established states, and RATS separates attes-
tation evidence and appraisal from a relying party’s authorization
decision [1, 3, 4, 11]. Our delta is an immutable execution-evidence
status paired with an explicit authorization basis, bound to deter-
ministic comparison and evaluated on real engines.
Proof carrying and translation validation.Proof-carrying
code attaches a machine-checkable safety proof, which our
execution-claim-carrying envelope does not provide [5]. Transla-
tion validation checks a particular output against source semantics,
while our case-specific comparison checks a verifier observation
against a signed engine-attributed observation and does not
validate any source-to-L translation [7].
Carrier-adjacent work.C2PA separates cryptographic hard
binding from soft fingerprints or watermarks, while QRscript
shows that executable bytecode can be carried by standard visual
symbols [2, 8]. We require exact envelope recovery followed by
signature verification and make no carrier novelty claim.

11 Discussion
11.1 What the execution commitment adds
A signed expected result is an attributed comparison point, not
proof or fault diagnosis. A0 carries only the program. A1 authen-
ticates the publisher’s claim but establishes no agreement. B/C de-
tected every observation-divergent stale-3 claim. Under B/C, the
controlled backend fault and semantics drift were missed at same-
artifact I0 and exposed at I1–I3, while the Rust-runtime common-
mode fault was missed at I1/I2 and exposed only by E4 at I3. E1→E4
separates runtime backend and build toolchain but shares translit-
erated source, parser, and semantic-core lineage. Agreement there-
fore shows case-specific compatibility, not historical execution, di-
agnosis, source-independent replication, or defect exclusion.
11.2 Policy usability
The explicit origin-only path avoids undocumented bypasses
for unsupported or resource-limited artifacts.not_reexecuted
preserves its four reasons, andnot_comparable(reason)has
the same typed option after an impossible comparison. Neither
becomes execution agreement. Complete current-report aggrega-
tion prevents skipping or cherry-picking from defeating a later
disagreement.
11.3 Channel and carrier independence
We evaluated a representative lossy visual carrier, rejected a cus-
tom design against a strong standard mosaic, and therefore use
standard symbols with sharding and outer erasure correction. Full
definitions and results are in the artifact.
11.4 From model to implementation
The artifact exhausts the finite join, reason, projection, status,
basis, scope-pin, and conjunctive-policy domains and catches
three mutations. It neither enumerates arbitrary predicates nor
proves implementation conformance. Structurally, any projected
predicate still guards only the grant from𝐴𝐼(𝐽)and cannot relabel
status. Implementations must centralize complete-set aggregation,
preserve immutable typed status and reasons, require references
for verified provenance, pin verifier scope, and test canonical en-
coding, parser differences, signature misuse, resource exhaustion,
report ordering, and the mutations.
12 Conclusion
We make the separation of authenticated origin from authority ex-
plicit with split signed types and a typedVerificationStatus×
AuthorizationBasisover complete report aggregates. Policy can-
not rewrite evidence, and disagreement supersedes fallback. High-
authority independence requires referenced, verified-provenance
Distinctaxes, so a signer cannot promote its own assertion. The
finite model catches three laundering mutations but is safety ev-
idence for the abstraction, not an implementation-conformance
proof.
The real-L matrix passed160/160 condition cells, reproduced
all artifacts byte-for-byte, and producedbyte-identical canoni-
cal transcriptsfor four programs over five paths. Observation-
divergent stale-3 wasN/Aat A0, missed at A1, and detected
throughout B/C. Under B/C the backend fault was missed at I0
and caught at I1–I3. The Rust-runtime common-mode fault was
missed at I1/I2 and caught by E4 at I3. Because E1→E4 shares
transliterated source and semantic-core lineage, that cell measures
backend separation only.
This bounded corpus has four clean programs, six fixtures,
84/205hosted primitive rows, and34/144broader historical
agreeing cases. The 160 cells are conditions, not programs. Raw
observations differ, exhaustion equivalence is unproved, scope
C lacks a comparable language-level step trace, and all paths
share one operator, organization, machine, and specification.
Conformance-scale coverage, a non-transliterated implementa-
tion, independent operation, and physical-channel replication
remain future work. No whole-language or source-independent
result is claimed.
